This is the MOI-agnostic BASE score for this variant — it covers only
variant-intrinsic evidence (mechanism, frequency, in-silico scores, functional
studies, phenotype specificity). It deliberately does NOT include de novo
status (PS2) or family cosegregation (PP1) — those are mode-of-inheritance-
dependent and are scored separately by downstream MOI-specific analyses (de
novo, dominant-inherited, recessive, X-linked), which ADD their own criteria
on top of this base score. Do not attempt to apply PS2 or PP1 here even if
the evidence suggests them.

PHENOTYPE PERTINENCE CHECK (mandatory, check before writing Phenotype fit):
If PRIOR REASONING contains a "GENE-PHENOTYPE LITERATURE PERTINENCE
(backend-determined)" block stating no gene-phenotype literature link was
found, Phenotype fit CANNOT be "match" — set it to "mismatch" or "uncertain"
regardless of what the rest of the reasoning text implies, and do not apply
PVS1 (its gate below depends on Phenotype fit). A backend value of "positive
link found" supports but does not by itself guarantee "match" — still ground
the specific mechanism/phenotype fit judgment in the evidence as before. If
that block is absent or says "unknown", judge Phenotype fit from the evidence
as before, unconstrained.

HEDGED-LANGUAGE CHECK (mandatory, applies regardless of the backend block
above — read the literature evidence's own wording, not just its verdict):
A real, worked past failure: patient's phenotype was "visual impairment"; a
gene's literature evidence explicitly stated its disease is intellectual
disability + epilepsy + autism, then added "documented sensory abnormalities
... suggest significant potential for visual processing deficits within the
broader sensory phenotype" — and this hedge alone was read as "Phenotype fit:
match", unlocking PVS1 and producing a Pathogenic call for the wrong gene.
Phrases like "may indicate", "suggests potential for", "could plausibly
include", "within the broader phenotype", "cannot be ruled out" are NOT an
explicit phenotype match — they are the literature-summary tool's own
inference, not a stated finding. If the evidence's own gene-disease
description names a DIFFERENT core phenotype than the patient's (e.g.
intellectual disability/epilepsy/autism vs. this patient's visual
impairment), Phenotype fit is "mismatch" or "uncertain" — never "match" —
regardless of any hedged bridging language layered on top. Only an
explicitly, directly stated association between this gene/variant and the
patient's actual presenting phenotype counts as "match".

RECESSIVE SINGLE-HIT CHECK (mandatory, check before writing Inheritance check):
If PRIOR REASONING states the gene inheritance mode is autosomal recessive (AR)
or X-linked recessive (XLR), this variant is heterozygous, AND PRIOR
REASONING's compound-het check row says "No second variant in gene" (no
sibling variant present, and this variant itself is not homozygous) — this
variant ALONE is insufficient to explain a recessive phenotype, no matter how
strong its individual ACMG evidence (PVS1, high CADD, etc.) looks. In that
case: set Inheritance check to "insufficient data — recessive gene, single
heterozygous hit, no second variant identified", and state explicitly in the
Comment that this variant cannot be concluded as causative on its own under
this recessive model. Do not describe the variant as causative, explanatory
of the phenotype, or diagnostic by itself in this scenario.

ACMG criteria guidance (point value in brackets, per Tavtigian et al. 2020 Bayesian point system):
- PVS1 [VeryStrong, +8]: Loss-of-function variant in a gene where LoF is a known disease mechanism (use AutoPVS1 output when available). Two independent gates, BOTH required:
  (1) THIS VARIANT'S OWN Type must itself be a LoF consequence (nonsense, frameshift, canonical ±1/2 splice site, initiation codon loss, or a deletion removing critical exons/whole gene) — check the Type/HGVS fields directly. "GNOMAD GENE CONSTRAINT" (pLI/LOEUF) is GENE-level evidence about whether the gene tolerates LoF in general — it can support that LoF is a plausible mechanism for the gene, but a high pLI/low LOEUF NEVER by itself makes a non-LoF variant (missense, synonymous, etc.) eligible for PVS1. If Type is not LoF, do not apply PVS1 regardless of how LoF-intolerant the gene is.
  (2) Phenotype fit above must be "match" — a LoF mechanism alone does not justify PVS1 if the gene is not linked to the patient's phenotype (Phenotype fit "mismatch" or "uncertain"). If phenotype fit is not a match, do not apply PVS1 regardless of AutoPVS1 strength; note the mechanism as unconfirmed disease relevance instead.
  PLI/LOEUF CAVEAT — CHECK THE GENE'S INHERITANCE MODE FIRST, before using pLI/LOEUF as a caveat: pLI/LOEUF measure selection against a HETEROZYGOUS single LoF allele in the general population — that is only informative about a DOMINANT/haploinsufficiency-based reading of this variant (i.e. this single LoF hit being sufficient to cause disease on its own). It says NOTHING about biallelic (homozygous or compound-het) pathogenicity.
    - If the gene's inheritance mode (per PRIOR REASONING's "Gene inheritance mode" row) is autosomal recessive (AR) or X-linked recessive (XLR), AND this variant is homozygous or has a confirmed/likely compound-het partner (biallelic) — do NOT cite pLI/LOEUF as a caveat against PVS1 or against pathogenicity at all. A LOW pLI / HIGH LOEUF (LoF-tolerant) is EXPECTED and NORMAL for a true recessive gene — population carriers of a single LoF allele are unaffected by definition in recessive disease, so gnomAD's heterozygous-constraint signal is uninformative here, not contradictory evidence. A real past failure to not repeat: a homozygous/compound-het LoF call in a bona fide AR gene had its Comment state "the gene is LoF-tolerant, suggesting loss-of-function variants are... less likely to be highly penetrant in a recessive manner" — this is backwards; LoF-tolerance is precisely what a true recessive gene's population data should look like, and must never be used to downweight a biallelic finding.
    - Only when this variant is being relied on as a SINGLE heterozygous LoF hit sufficient to explain the phenotype by itself (dominant-relevant gene mode, or an AR/XLR gene with no second hit where the variant is being considered as a potential dominant/incompletely-recessive exception) does the pLI/LOEUF caveat apply: pLI≥0.9 or LOEUF<0.35 (LoF-intolerant/constrained) supports LoF as a real disease mechanism for this gene; a low pLI or high LOEUF (LoF-tolerant) argues against a single heterozygous LoF hit being sufficient, and must be noted as a caveat in the Comment even when PVS1 is still applied on other grounds.
- PS1 [Strong, +4]: Same amino acid change as a previously established pathogenic variant. PRIMARY SOURCE: the "CLINVAR RESIDUE-LEVEL SEARCH" evidence block above is a deterministic ClinVar query for every classified variant at this variant's own residue — it already tags each hit "SAME amino acid change as this variant" or "DIFFERENT amino acid change than this variant" and flags which are [PATHOGENIC/LIKELY PATHOGENIC]. Apply PS1 when a hit is tagged SAME amino acid change, is flagged pathogenic/likely pathogenic, AND its nucleotide (the "c." notation shown) differs from this variant's own — cite both nucleotide changes side by side (e.g. "candidate c.3962G>C vs. established c.3961T>A, both p.Cys1321Ser"). A hit tagged SAME amino acid change with the IDENTICAL nucleotide to this variant is this variant's own ClinVar record, not a separate allele — citing it as PS1 is circular reasoning; check PS3 instead (see below). A hit tagged DIFFERENT amino acid change is PM5's territory (below), never PS1's. Do NOT apply PS1 if the differing nucleotide is simply the reverse complement of this variant's own at the same genomic position — that is the same underlying event on the opposite strand, not an independent change.
  FALLBACK (only if the CLINVAR RESIDUE-LEVEL SEARCH block is absent for this variant): the "VARIANT WEB SEARCH" block also searches by residue and may surface a relevant hit. Manually verify: destination amino acid identical letter-by-letter (not just residue number), AND nucleotide differs, AND the hit is explicitly established as Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic in the evidence. Same residue number alone is never sufficient for PS1 — verify the amino acid itself.
- PS3 [Strong, +4]: Well-established in vitro or in vivo functional studies supporting a damaging effect. TRIGGER: the "CLINVAR VARIANT-LEVEL SUBMISSION TALLY" block above (present for every variant with a resolvable ClinVar record, not only when ClinVar_class already reads Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic) escalates, when submitters disagree on classification, into a "CLINVAR CONFLICTING — PATHOGENIC/LIKELY PATHOGENIC SUBMISSION EVIDENCE REVIEW" block listing each individual P/LP submission's own rationale. Apply PS3 whenever at least one of those per-submission lines is tagged "[mentions functional/experimental evidence]" — that tag alone is sufficient grounding, cite the PMID(s) listed on that same line. If the tally block is present but shows unanimous/non-conflicting agreement (no escalated evidence-review section), or no submission is tagged, or the ID could not be resolved, do NOT apply PS3 on the strength of the aggregate ClinVar classification alone (P/LP classification by itself is not PS3 — PS3 requires the underlying functional data, never just the label). A "CLINVAR RESIDUE-LEVEL SEARCH" or "VARIANT WEB SEARCH" hit (see PS1 above) that turns out to share this variant's own nucleotide change (not a different residue-level precedent) is this variant's own record found by a different route — treat it the same way: usable for PS3 only if it itself cites functional/experimental data, not merely a classification label. When multiple functional studies conflict, weight the most recent publication (highest year in the [PMID:XXXXXXXX, YEAR] citation) more heavily and note the disagreement rather than silently picking one. Also state the tally's classification counts explicitly in the Comment whenever the record is conflicting (e.g. "Pathogenic=3, Likely pathogenic=7, Uncertain significance=5") rather than treating the aggregate label as unanimous.
- PS4 [Strong, +4]: The prevalence of the variant in affected individuals is significantly increased over its prevalence in controls. Only apply this criterion if the evidence above states an explicit count of affected individuals/patients/probands/families reported with this exact variant (e.g. "identified in 12 unrelated probands", "observed in 3 of 40 patients") — cite that number alongside the judgment. A qualitative mechanism statement alone (e.g. "a known mutational hotspot", "recurrent variant") is NOT sufficient grounding for PS4 without an actual headcount — that supports PM1 (hotspot/domain) instead, not PS4. If no explicit affected-individual count is present in the evidence, do NOT apply PS4.
- PM1 [Moderate, +2]: Located in a mutational hot spot and/or critical and well-established functional domain. Only apply if the evidence above explicitly names a specific domain, motif, or hotspot AND states this variant's residue/position falls within it (e.g. a named functional domain from AutoPVS1/UniProt/literature, or literature explicitly describing this residue as a recurrent/hotspot position). General statements about the gene's overall importance, constraint (pLI/LOEUF), or disease association are NOT sufficient grounding for PM1 — those support PVS1/PP4 mechanism plausibility, not domain/hotspot location specifically. If no such named domain/hotspot evidence exists for this variant's exact position, do NOT apply PM1.
- PM2 [Moderate, +2]: Absent from controls in population databases (gnomAD, ExAC) or at very low frequency. Check this independently of de novo/PS2 status — always report the Frequency field. If the variant's own Frequency field is NA/blank, a "GNOMAD VARIANT FREQUENCY" evidence block above is a live fallback lookup — use its actual AF value if present (do NOT apply PM2 from "Frequency is blank, therefore presumed rare" reasoning when this block reports a real, non-negligible allele frequency; a blank CSV field is not itself evidence of rarity). If that block also reports no match/no coverage in gnomAD, that IS a legitimate absence-of-data signal — apply PM2 cautiously in that case, noting explicitly that it rests on absence from gnomAD rather than a directly observed low frequency.
- PM5 [Moderate, +2]: Novel missense change at an amino acid residue where a DIFFERENT missense pathogenic variant has been documented at the SAME residue — same residue number, but a DIFFERENT destination amino acid than this variant's own, regardless of nucleotide change. PRIMARY SOURCE: the "CLINVAR RESIDUE-LEVEL SEARCH" evidence block above already tags each hit at this residue — apply PM5 when a hit is tagged "DIFFERENT amino acid change than this variant" AND flagged [PATHOGENIC/LIKELY PATHOGENIC]; cite that hit's own change (e.g. established p.Cys1321Gly vs. this variant's own p.Cys1321Ser). A hit tagged "SAME amino acid change" is PS1's territory (above), never PM5's. Worked example: candidate p.Pro334Ser vs. an established Pathogenic p.Pro334Leu — same residue 334, Ser vs. Leu are different amino acids → PM5, not PS1 (residue number matching is not amino-acid matching).
  FALLBACK (only if the CLINVAR RESIDUE-LEVEL SEARCH block is absent for this variant): the "VARIANT WEB SEARCH" block also searches by residue and may surface a relevant hit — scan it for a change at the same position with a DIFFERENT resulting amino acid than this variant's own, explicitly classified Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic in the evidence. Only apply PM5 when such a distinct, differently-substituted allele is explicitly named — never from residue plausibility alone. PM5 and PS1 may both apply to the same variant if separate hits support each.
- PP3 [Supporting, +1]: Multiple lines of computational evidence support a deleterious effect. Only apply this criterion if the evidence above contains actual named in-silico tool scores (e.g. REVEL, CADD, SIFT, PolyPhen-2, AlphaMissense, SpliceAI). If no in-silico score is present in the evidence, do NOT apply PP3 — a vague "computational evidence supports pathogenicity" without a named tool and score is not sufficient grounding for this criterion.
  When scores ARE present, evaluate each one against its standard pathogenicity threshold below (do not just report the number — judge whether it crosses the deleterious threshold):
    - REVEL: ≥0.7 supports deleterious; ≤0.3 supports benign; in between is inconclusive.
    - CADD (Phred-scaled): ≥20 supports deleterious (top ~1% most damaging); below is weaker/inconclusive.
    - SIFT: <0.05 supports deleterious ("damaging"); ≥0.05 supports tolerated/benign.
    - PolyPhen-2: >0.85 supports deleterious ("probably damaging"); 0.15–0.85 possibly damaging/inconclusive; <0.15 supports benign.
    - AlphaMissense: >0.564 supports deleterious ("likely pathogenic"); <0.34 supports benign ("likely benign"); in between is ambiguous.
    - SpliceAI (delta score): ≥0.5 supports a splice-altering effect; <0.2 does not.
  Apply PP3 only when the reported scores actually cross their deleterious threshold — cite each tool's name and score value (e.g. "REVEL=0.82, CADD=28.3") alongside the judgment. If a score falls in the inconclusive/benign range, say so explicitly and do not count it toward PP3.
- PP4 [Supporting, +1]: Patient's phenotype or family history is highly specific for a disease with a single known genetic etiology. Two conditions must BOTH hold — do not apply PP4 on either one alone:
  (1) Single-gene etiology: the evidence indicates this disease/phenotype is caused by variants in ONE gene (not genetically heterogeneous — i.e. not a condition where multiple different genes are each independently known to cause the same phenotype). If the evidence shows or does not rule out genetic heterogeneity, do NOT apply PP4 — state the heterogeneity explicitly instead. A real past failure to not repeat: PP4 was applied for a gene X variant in a patient with "hypercholesterolemia and an affected mother" reasoning "specific for familial hypercholesterolemia caused by gene X variants" — but familial hypercholesterolemia is a textbook genetically HETEROGENEOUS disease (LDLR ~85-90% of cases, but APOB and PCSK9 independently cause the identical phenotype too) — condition (1) fails here regardless of how classic the phenotype looks, and PP4 should not have been applied. Before applying PP4, actively check whether the evidence names other genes for the same disease (as this pipeline's own gene evidence table often does, by listing multiple candidate genes for the same phenotype in one run) — if it does, that alone rules out single-gene etiology.
  (2) Discriminating specificity: the patient's phenotype must include at least one feature that is itself a recognized hallmark of this specific disease, not merely a generic symptom this disease happens to share with many other conditions. A broad, non-discriminating overlap (e.g. "intellectual disability", "developmental delay", "seizures", "short stature" in isolation) is NOT sufficient — those are common to hundreds of syndromes and do not distinguish this disease from its differentials. Example of a genuinely specific match: micropenis in a patient being evaluated for Kallmann syndrome — micropenis reflects the congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism that defines Kallmann and is not an incidental or generic finding. Name the exact feature(s) driving the match and state explicitly why they are distinctive for this disease rather than merely present in it. If the only overlap is nonspecific/generic features, do NOT apply PP4 — say so explicitly instead of applying it on general plausibility.
  A real past failure to not repeat: the patient's ENTIRE phenotype input was the single generic term "intellectual disability" (nothing else — no additional features, no family history, no specific findings) and PP4 was applied anyway with the justification "a core feature of the [GENE]-associated syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder" — this is circular (every candidate gene in the whole run shares this same generic phenotype; being a "core feature" of a broad syndrome name is not the same as being a discriminating feature) and directly violates this condition's own listed example. When the patient's phenotype consists of ONLY a generic term like this with no further distinguishing detail, PP4's condition (2) fails by construction — do not apply PP4 for ANY gene in that run on phenotype grounds, no matter how strong the gene-disease literature link otherwise looks. PP4 needs a specific finding to point to; a single generic diagnosis label is not one.
  Regardless of how phenotype-specific the match is, the point value used for PP4 must always stay below 5 (Supporting [+1] by default; Moderate [+2] at most if evidence strongly justifies it — never Strong [+4] or higher).
- BA1 [Standalone Benign, -8]: Allele frequency > 5% in population databases.
- BS1 [Strong Benign, -4]: Allele frequency greater than expected for disorder. This requires an explicit comparison between this variant's Frequency and a stated disease prevalence/expected-carrier-frequency figure — the evidence above generally does NOT contain disease-prevalence data (no tool in this pipeline fetches it), so BS1 will usually have no valid grounding and should NOT be applied. Do not apply BS1 merely because the frequency "seems too common" for a rare disease, or from gene/disease familiarity — that is a plausibility judgment, not cited evidence. Only apply if the evidence above explicitly states a disease prevalence or expected-frequency figure to compare against.
- BS4 [Strong Benign, -4]: Lack of segregation with disease in affected family members. Do not apply this to outweigh a strong LoF/PVS1 finding without explicitly reconciling the conflict in the Comment.
- BP6 [Supporting, -1]: A reputable source (ClinVar) reports THIS variant's own classification as Likely benign or Benign. Apply from the **ClinVar:** field above (i.e. this variant's own ClinVar_class, not a different variant/residue) reading Likely benign or Benign. Unlike BS3/PS3, BP6 does NOT require functional-study data — the classification label itself is sufficient grounding; do not withhold BP6 merely because no functional evidence is cited, that is the entire point of this criterion (a reputable source's call, taken on trust, precisely when independent functional evaluation is unavailable). Do not apply if ClinVar_class is Uncertain significance, Pathogenic, Likely pathogenic, or "Conflicting interpretations" (including any Conflicting classification that contains a P/LP submission) without explicitly addressing the conflict in the Comment — a genuinely conflicted record is not a reputable unambiguous benign call.
- PP2 / BP1 — CHECK THIS VARIANT'S OWN Type FIELD FIRST, before reading the verdict: PP2 and BP1 both require this variant's own Type to be missense. The "CLINVAR GENE-LEVEL P/LP VARIANT COUNTS" block's verdict ("missense-predominant" / "nonsense/frameshift-predominant") is a GENE-level fact — true for every variant in that gene regardless of what Type this specific variant is — it is NOT itself a signal that PP2 or BP1 applies to this variant. Worked negative example (a real past failure — do not repeat it): a homozygous NONSENSE variant in a gene with verdict "nonsense/frameshift-predominant" is NOT "a nonsense variant in a truncating-predominant gene, supporting BP1" — that is backwards. A nonsense variant in a truncating-predominant gene is a mechanism MATCH (PVS1 territory, since LoF is confirmed to be how this gene causes disease), never BP1. BP1 fires only when the CANDIDATE ITSELF is missense despite the gene being truncating-predominant (a mechanism MISMATCH, arguing against pathogenicity) — if Type is anything other than missense (nonsense, frameshift, splice, etc.), BP1 cannot apply no matter what the verdict says, full stop.
  - PP2 [Supporting, +1]: Missense variant in a gene with a low rate of benign missense variation and where missense variants are a common mechanism of disease. Only apply if this variant's Type is missense AND verdict is "missense-predominant" — do not apply from gene name, general plausibility, or the verdict text alone without first confirming Type is missense.
  - BP1 [Supporting, -1]: Missense variant in a gene for which primarily truncating variants are known to cause disease. Only apply if this variant's Type is missense AND verdict is "nonsense/frameshift-predominant" — do not apply from gene name, general plausibility, or the verdict text alone without first confirming Type is missense. PP2 and BP1 are mutually exclusive for the same variant.
- BP7 [Supporting, -1]: A synonymous variant for which no splicing disruption is predicted. Only apply if this variant's own Type is synonymous — never apply to missense, nonsense, frameshift, or splice-region variants regardless of their SpliceAI scores. When Type is synonymous, check the "SpliceAI" evidence block for this variant: cite its actual delta scores (DS_AG/AL/DG/DL) as the basis for "no splicing disruption predicted" (all deltas <0.2, per the PP3 SpliceAI threshold above) — do not assert "no disruption predicted" without citing that block. If no SpliceAI evidence is present for this variant, do NOT apply BP7.

Evaluate each criterion based on the evidence in the context. Only list criteria that are supported by evidence or that can be ruled out with evidence.

Classification thresholds (sum of applied points):
- Pathogenic: ≥ 10
- Likely Pathogenic: 6 to 9
- Uncertain Significance (VUS): 0 to 5
- Likely Benign: -1 to -6
- Benign: ≤ -7
