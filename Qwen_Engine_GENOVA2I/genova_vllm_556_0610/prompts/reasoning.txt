TASK — DETAILED REASONING:

Reason step by step using ONLY the provided evidence. Every factual claim must be
traceable to a specific reference present in the evidence above (e.g. PMID, URL,

Reasoning steps:

1. Phenotype fit: Identify any gene–disease related assertions present in the provided
   evidence and assess their consistency with the patient's clinical features. Quote or cite
   the relevant context passage (e.g. "LitVar2 reports [PMID:XXXXX]…", "AutoPVS1 output states…").
   Do NOT assert gene–disease links that are not explicitly present in the provided evidence.

   A real patient's phenotype text often lists findings from more than one distinct
   system/cluster (e.g. a neurodevelopmental cluster — epilepsy, intellectual
   disability, autism — AND a separate cluster — an organ malformation, a cancer, a
   dysmorphic feature). Before writing your prose assessment, output ONE labeled
   verdict line, in exactly this form:

     PHENOTYPE CLUSTER MATCH: YES | PARTIAL | NO

   This is the single most important line in this entire step — a later pipeline
   stage extracts it verbatim and treats it as final, so get it right and do not
   hedge, qualify, or omit it. Definitions:
     YES     — the evidence affirmatively links this gene to the patient's phenotype
               essentially as a whole (no separate cluster is left unaddressed).
     PARTIAL — the evidence affirmatively links this gene to AT LEAST ONE distinct
               cluster/feature of the patient's phenotype, but is silent on at
               least one other cluster the patient also has. This is a POSITIVE,
               sufficient gene-phenotype link for that matched cluster — it is
               NOT a weaker or borderline case of NO, and downstream stages must
               treat it the same as YES for inclusion purposes. Genetic syndromes
               are pleiotropic and variably-expressive; the literature not
               mentioning every finding in a multi-system phenotype is normal and
               expected, not a red flag.
     NO      — the evidence names no condition/category that overlaps ANY part of
               the patient's phenotype, OR it explicitly, affirmatively states the
               gene does NOT cause the patient's phenotype.
   PARTIAL is the common, correct answer for a broad or multi-system phenotype and
   a gene with real but incomplete literature support — do not default to NO just
   because coverage isn't full. A citation naming a broad category (e.g.
   "epilepsy," "autism spectrum disorder," "intellectual disability,"
   "cardiomyopathy") that the patient's phenotype text also contains — even in
   different words, even if the citation is silent on every other finding in the
   patient's list, even if the citation itself says it doesn't cover the patient's
   other features — is PARTIAL (or YES if it's genuinely the patient's whole
   picture), never NO. The citation not mentioning a symptom is evidence about
   that symptom only — it is NOT evidence against the symptom(s) the citation DOES
   name.

   WORKED EXAMPLE (apply this reasoning pattern):
     Patient phenotype: "cognitive deficit, fine motor impairment, autism spectrum
     disorder, epilepsy since age 2.5-3, partial alopecia, hypotelorism, frontal
     bossing, high palate, V-shaped maxilla, facial hypotonia, pectus excavatum."
     Evidence: "Mutations in the three synapsin genes [including this gene] lead to
     profound social deficits and cortical dysfunctions... associated with epilepsy
     and autism spectrum disorders" but does not mention the craniofacial or
     skeletal features.
     WRONG: "PHENOTYPE CLUSTER MATCH: NO — the evidence does not cover the
     craniofacial features, specific motor deficits, or age of onset, so there is
     no gene-phenotype link." (Treats partial coverage as no match — forbidden.)
     RIGHT: "PHENOTYPE CLUSTER MATCH: PARTIAL — the evidence affirmatively links
     this gene to epilepsy and autism spectrum disorder, both present in this
     patient (epilepsy since 2.5-3y, ASD). The evidence is silent on the
     craniofacial/skeletal cluster (alopecia, hypotelorism, frontal bossing,
     V-shaped maxilla, pectus excavatum); silence does not contradict them."
   A genuine "PHENOTYPE CLUSTER MATCH: NO" looks like this instead: gene is
   evidenced only for an isolated cardiac arrhythmia with no neurodevelopmental,
   dysmorphic, or skeletal association anywhere in the literature, and the
   patient's phenotype is pure neurodevelopmental/dysmorphic with no cardiac
   finding at all — zero overlap on any named category, not merely incomplete
   detail on a category that does overlap.

   After the verdict line, write your prose Phenotype Fit assessment. If the
   verdict is YES or PARTIAL, state plainly that the gene explains the matched
   cluster and that the evidence is silent on — not contradicting — any remaining
   cluster. Do NOT write "gap," "mismatch," "not supported," or similar language
   as the conclusion when the verdict is YES or PARTIAL — reserve that language
   for a genuine NO verdict.

4. Inheritance coherence: Evaluate whether the observed zygosity is consistent with
   the gene's known inheritance mode. Use only the inheritance data present in the
   context (variant fields or retrieved evidence).
   Zygosity determination: the variant's "Zygosity" field is frequently "NA" — this
   does NOT mean zygosity is unknown, it means you must derive it yourself from the
   ALLELIC BALANCE block (if present). Use the proband's own allelic balance value:
     - Proband AB roughly 0.3-0.7 → heterozygous
     - Proband AB above ~0.8 (approaching 1.0) → homozygous
     - Proband AB near 0 → variant not actually present / call artifact
   Apply this same derivation to any sibling variant's zygosity too (using the
   sibling's own AB values in its evidence block, not this variant's AB) — do not
   report a variant's zygosity as "NA" or "unknown" when its allelic balance data
   is available; state the AB value and the zygosity you derive from it.
   If a "SIBLING VARIANT(S) IN SAME GENE" block is present below the evidence, this
   patient carries another variant in this same gene and the gene's mode is
   autosomal recessive (or AD/AR) — evaluate this variant jointly with the sibling
   rather than in isolation: note the sibling's HGVS, zygosity, and evidence, and
   state whether the pair is consistent with a compound heterozygous or homozygous
   recessive presentation. Phase (CIS/TRANS/UNKNOWN) is a backend-determined fact —
   copy it verbatim from the sibling block's "Phase vs this variant
   (backend-determined): ..." line. Do NOT compute CIS/TRANS yourself from the raw
   allelic-balance numbers in the evidence — treat the backend line as the sole
   source of truth for phase, even if your own reading of the AB numbers suggests
   otherwise.
   HARD RULE: if the backend-determined phase is CIS (same parent), the pair is NOT
   compound heterozygous — one parental allele carries both variants, the other
   parental allele is wild-type, so this does not satisfy a biallelic/recessive
   model. State this explicitly and do not describe the pair as compound
   heterozygous in that case.

5. Call confidence & parental segregation:
   - Proband allelic balance (own AB, from the Allelic_balance field or "Proband:"
     in the ALLELIC BALANCE block): AB ≤ 0.2 is weak/uncertain support for a
     heterozygous call (possible low-level mosaicism, sequencing artifact, or
     allele dropout). Flag this explicitly — do not treat the variant as a
     confirmed het call without noting the low-AB caveat.
   - If ALLELIC BALANCE data is present, evaluate the variant's inheritance pattern:
     - De novo: AB near 0 in both parents (variant absent in parents)
     - Inherited from one parent: AB ~0.5 in one parent, ~0 in the other
     - Both parents carriers: AB ~0.5 in both parents
     - Homozygous parent: AB near 1.0 in one parent
     - Note how this affects ACMG evaluation — specifically PS2 (de novo) requires
       confirmed de novo with both parents tested and confirmed not to carry the
       variant. PM6 (assumed de novo) applies when one parent is unavailable or
       untested.
   - De novo status is INDEPENDENT of population frequency (PM2/PS2 are separate
     criteria). Always check the Frequency field regardless of de novo status.
     A de novo call on a variant that is NOT rare (AF above ~0.1%) is internally
     inconsistent for a dominant Mendelian disease — flag this contradiction
     explicitly rather than treating "de novo" alone as sufficient pathogenicity
     evidence.
   If no ALLELIC BALANCE data is present, state that parental segregation and
   proband call confidence cannot be assessed from AB.

6. Flags and uncertainties: Note any contradictions, gaps, or missing evidence
   (e.g. no population frequency data, no literature hits, failed tool). Be explicit
   about what information is absent and how that affects confidence.
