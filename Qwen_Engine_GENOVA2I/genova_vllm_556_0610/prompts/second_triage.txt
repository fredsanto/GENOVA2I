Decide whether this variant should proceed to the final clinical conclusion.
INCLUDE only variants with real clinical significance: a clear gene-phenotype link

Variants included here appear in the final clinical report — be selective.

       Your Exclude-case line MUST NOT contain phenotype-linkage language at
       all in this case — no "no gene-phenotype link," "not linked to,"

       paraphrase of these. If you catch yourself writing any such phrase
       after seeing a YES/PARTIAL block, delete it — Exclude-case may only
       cite criterion-2 (pathogenicity) grounds in that case, or a truly
       independent, separate track of evidence you can name and quote. A
       PARTIAL verdict is not itself such grounds — it is criterion 1's own

     - Block says NO -> criterion 1 is NOT satisfied on phenotype grounds;
       proceed to EXCLUDE unless a different track of evidence (e.g. a

     - Block says UNKNOWN, or the block is entirely absent (older-format

   - Gene reported in literature or OMIM for this phenotype or a phenotypically
     overlapping condition, OR
   - ClinVar entries for this gene list the patient's disease as associated condition.
   A literature hit only counts if it describes an actual gene-disease relationship
   (this gene causes/is associated with this disease or a genuine differential of it)
   — NOT a paper that merely contains a shared descriptive word while describing an
   unrelated disease process. E.g. a paper about hypermethylation of gene X in
   "vascular cognitive impairment" that happens to mention "visual and cognitive
   deficits" as a symptom of that acquired/vascular condition is NOT a gene-phenotype
   link for a patient being worked up for a primary genetic visual impairment — that
   is keyword overlap, not a disease relationship, and does not satisfy criterion 1.
   If the literature evidence itself states there is no direct link to the patient's
   specific phenotype (even while discussing the gene in some other disease context),
   treat criterion 1 as NOT met.

   Do not translate one evidence track's silence into an explicit denial. "Literature
   explicitly denies this gene causes the phenotype" is a much stronger claim than
   "this particular search/abstract set did not confirm a match" — only use denial
   language when a source directly and affirmatively states the gene does NOT cause
   the phenotype (rare). If the variant record includes multiple literature tracks
   (e.g. a gene-level PubMed block AND a separate LitVar2 variant-level block), a
   phenotype match reported in EITHER block satisfies criterion 1 for that gene, even
   if only reported at the cohort/gene level rather than confirmed for this specific
   variant, and even if a different track for the same gene found only unrelated
   conditions. A real past failure: a gene's own LitVar2 variant-level evidence
   reported a cohort of patients with optic atrophy (a visual-impairment phenotype)
   attributed to variants in that gene, but because the separate gene-level PubMed
   track had turned up only unrelated neurodevelopmental-disorder papers, the case
   was EXCLUDEd with "literature explicitly denies [gene] causes visual impairment" —
   this is backwards: the optic-atrophy finding satisfies criterion 1 on its own; a
   different track finding no match does not cancel it out. Cite the matching finding
   and note the variant-specific gap (not confirmed for this exact variant) as a
   caveat in the Exclude-case text, never as grounds to claim no gene-phenotype link
   exists at all.

   PARTIAL PHENOTYPE OVERLAP IS SUFFICIENT — do not require the literature to
   explain every listed clinical feature. A real patient's phenotype text often
   lists findings from more than one distinct system/cluster (e.g. a
   neurodevelopmental cluster — epilepsy, intellectual disability, autism —
   AND a separate dysmorphic/skeletal cluster — specific facial features,
   pectus excavatum, alopecia). Genetic syndromes are pleiotropic and
   variably-expressive; one gene explaining the core/major cluster of the
   presenting phenotype satisfies criterion 1 even when the literature says
   nothing about, or does not cover, a separate secondary cluster the patient
   also has. Do NOT exclude on the reasoning "literature does not describe
   the FULL combination of [feature A, B, C, D...]" when the gene's own
   literature affirmatively confirms the core cluster (A, B) — that is a
   genuine, sufficient gene-phenotype link, not "no link." A real past
   failure: a patient's phenotype combined a neurodevelopmental cluster
   (cognitive deficit, autism, epilepsy) with a separate dysmorphic cluster
   (partial alopecia, hypotelorism, frontal bossing, high-arched palate,
   V-shaped maxilla, pectus excavatum). A candidate gene's own literature
   affirmatively confirmed the neurodevelopmental cluster — "mutations in
   this gene are associated with epilepsy and autism spectrum disorders" —
   but did not describe the dysmorphic features, so the variant was EXCLUDEd
   with "literature explicitly states [gene] does not cause this phenotype."
   This is wrong: the literature never denied anything — it confirmed the
   core cluster and was simply silent on the secondary one (the same
   silence-is-not-denial principle above applies here too), and a single rare
   LoF variant explaining even part of a multi-system phenotype is exactly
   the kind of finding this stage exists to keep, not discard. Only exclude
   on phenotype grounds when the gene's literature is silent on or
   contradicts EVERY cluster in the patient's phenotype, not merely a
   secondary one.

2. PROBABLE PATHOGENICITY — the variant itself is likely damaging:
   - ClinVar Pathogenic or Likely pathogenic — but a ClinVar P/LP call is only as
     strong as the disease it's actually paired with. Check the ClinVar "Conditions"
     field against the patient's phenotype: if Conditions is blank/"(none listed)",
     or lists a disease unrelated to the patient's presentation, this P/LP label does
     NOT satisfy criterion 2 for this patient — a variant can be genuinely pathogenic
     for a disease this patient does not have, which says nothing about causing the
     phenotype under evaluation here. Only let the ClinVar label count when its listed
     condition plausibly matches or overlaps the patient's phenotype. Also weigh review
     status: a classification from a single submitter with no assertion criteria
     (0-star / "no specific assertion criteria provided") is the weakest ClinVar tier —
     combined with an absent/unrelated condition, treat it as no real signal at all,
     not as grounds for INCLUDE. A multi-submitter or expert-panel-reviewed P/LP
     classification with a condition matching the patient's phenotype is the strong
     version of this signal, OR
   - Strong ACMG criterion applies (PVS1, PS1, PS3, or equivalent), OR
   - Variant is rare (AF < 0.1%) with predicted functional impact (LoF, splice
     disruption, or high CADD score ≥ 20) — ZYGOSITY MUST MATCH INHERITANCE MODE:
     for a known AUTOSOMAL RECESSIVE gene, a single heterozygous LoF variant with
     no second hit (no compound het, not homozygous) is INSUFFICIENT on its own —
     treat as weak evidence, not strong, OR
   - Variant is homozygous, or compound heterozygous with a second variant in the
     same gene, in a known recessive gene with low population frequency, OR
     the variant is a loss-of-function type (frameshift, nonsense, canonical
     splice) — a homozygous LoF variant in a phenotype-matched gene is a STRONG
     signal on its own. The input data's own stated Zygosity field (e.g.
     "hom") is authoritative for this check — do NOT require ALLELIC BALANCE
     data to confirm it before counting it. Missing/NA allelic balance means
     the zygosity call is simply unconfirmed by that one additional source, not
     that it is wrong or untrustworthy; it is a caveat to mention, never a
     reason by itself to discount a stated homozygous call or exclude the
     variant. GNOMAD GENE CONSTRAINT (pLI/LOEUF) is NEVER grounds to discount
     this signal either: pLI/LOEUF measure selection against a HETEROZYGOUS
     single LoF allele in the general population — that is only informative
     about a DOMINANT/haploinsufficiency reading (a single LoF hit sufficient
     to cause disease on its own). It says NOTHING about biallelic pathogenicity.
     A low pLI / high LOEUF ("LoF-tolerant") is EXPECTED and NORMAL for a true
     recessive gene — population carriers of one LoF allele are unaffected by
     definition, so this metric is uninformative here, not contradictory
     evidence. A real past failure: a homozygous frameshift in a gene the
     evidence directly linked to the patient's phenotype, with 316 ClinVar P/LP
     LoF entries for that gene, was EXCLUDEd for "gene is LoF-tolerant (low
     pLI); homozygous LoF insufficient without strong functional proof" — this
     is backwards and must never be repeated: do not cite pLI/LOEUF against a
     homozygous or compound-het LoF call, OR
   - Confirmed or assumed DE NOVO (PS2/PM6 — AB data shows absent in both parents,
     or one parent untested) in a gene relevant to the phenotype is a STRONG
     pathogenicity signal, regardless of variant type (missense, LoF, etc.) — rank
     this above an unresolved single het LoF in a recessive gene — BUT only if the
     variant is ALSO rare (AF < 0.1%). De novo status does not override frequency:
     a de novo call on a common variant is not a valid de novo pathogenic signal
     (likely artifact or AB misread) and must not be INCLUDEd on that basis alone, OR
   - INHERITED FROM AN AFFECTED PARENT, in a gene with any dominant-relevant mode
     (AD, AD_AR, XLD, XLD_XLR, or mode unknown/not yet excluded as dominant), is
     JUST AS STRONG a pathogenicity signal as de novo — do not treat de novo as the
     only acceptable positive segregation route. If ALLELIC BALANCE data or the
     patient phenotype text indicates this variant was inherited from a parent
     who is themselves affected/diagnosed with a phenotypically consistent
     condition (e.g. "mother affected" alongside an AB pattern matching maternal
     transmission), that is cosegregation with disease — a rare heterozygous
     variant in a dominant-relevant gene, inherited from an affected parent, is
     sufficient grounds for INCLUDE on its own; it does not additionally need to
     be de novo, homozygous, or paired with a second hit, OR
   - Variant or position reported as disease-causing in literature.

   Note: "no functional study" does NOT equal no pathogenicity support. Rarity,
   high CADD, homozygosity, confirmed de novo status, and strong gene-disease link
   are all positive signals even when no wet-lab data exists for this specific variant.
